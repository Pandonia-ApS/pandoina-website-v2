The laboratory: no separate page

Why not

The laboratory's meaning is sequential — it is what happens between the sample leaving your kitchen and the report arriving. Lift it out of the day and it becomes a facility page, which is the least interesting version of the most interesting fact.

Where it lives instead

A full chapter inside Sådan fungerer det, at #laboratoriet, on deep forest with three photographs and the address. Deep-linkable from the homepage and from Om Pandonia.

And a second story elsewhere

Om Pandonia carries why we built our own — a decision. Sådan fungerer det carries what happens inside it — a process. Two different jobs, no repeated paragraph.

Revisit if

Accreditation, equipment and quality-control content arrives and runs past ~600 words. At that point it earns a page. Today it would be a page with four placeholders on it.

Unresolved structural problem, carried from Phase 3: every CTA hands the visitor to system.easypractice.net at the moment of commitment. The Book page now does everything it can — full price ladder, what's included, what happens after — but the last click still leaves the brand. This is a platform decision, not a design one.

02 / Nav

Global navigation

Four editorial destinations and one action. Everything else is in the footer.

Desktop — 72px

The four

Hvad vi måler · Sådan fungerer det · Din rapport · Om Pandonia. They map to the four questions a first-time visitor actually has: what do you measure, what happens, what do I get, who are you.

Why Priser is not in it

Because Book is the price page. The primary CTA appears on every screen, so price is always one click away — and merging them removes a page whose only job was duplicating the other one. The footer carries a "Priser" link to /book for people who hunt that word.

Why FAQ sits in the utility group

It is a support destination, not a story. Grouping it with Log ind and EN at 12.5px keeps the four editorial items reading as one set.

Behaviour

Sticky, cream, no blur, no shrink, no shadow. A 1px bottom rule appears after 8px of scroll and nothing else changes. Active item carries a forest underline.

Mobile — 60px

Full-screen overlay

Deep forest, items set in the serif at 28–40px, CTA pinned at the bottom. It is the one moment mobile gets to feel like the brand rather than like a menu, and a white drawer of 15px links throws that away.

No sticky CTA bar

The persistent bottom bar is a conversion pattern and it is generic. The primary CTA already recurs at the end of each act.

Escape and focus

Closes on Escape, the trigger carries aria-expanded, focus returns to the trigger. Implemented in the prototype.

04 / Journeys

How people move

Four routes, each carried by a link that belongs to the content rather than by a CTA that interrupts it.

Forside→Hvad vi måler→Område→Markør→Book

Beat 04 features one marker and ends "Se alle markører →". On Hvad vi måler the index bar carries you to an area; a marker row expands in place; the area footer offers the model. The only CTA on the whole page sits after the limits section. Curiosity is never interrupted — it is allowed to arrive.

Forside→Sådan fungerer det→Din rapport→Book

Beat 06 ends "Hele forløbet, trin for trin →". The process page's report chapter ends "Se hele rapporten →". Din rapport ends with the consultation and the primary CTA. Three pages, three links, each one answering the question the previous page just raised.

Forside→Laboratoriet→Om Pandonia→Book

Beat 09 links directly into #laboratoriet. From there the sceptic wants to know who these people are — Om Pandonia answers with the decision, the model and the team, and closes on the invitation. This is the credibility route and it carries no CTA until the very end.

Søgning→Markør→Område→Din rapport→Book

Someone googling "ferritin referenceområde" lands on a signature marker page, which sits inside its area. The area footer leads to the model; the page close leads to Din rapport. A cold visitor arriving at the deepest point still has a path out into the story — which is the whole reason the six signature markers get real URLs.

The rule: every page ends with one primary CTA and one editorial link that continues the argument. No page carries more than two primary CTAs. Nothing interrupts a reader mid-section.

Four voice rules

  1. State, don't sell

    "Vi kommer hjem til dig i København" — not "Oplev fremtidens sundhedstjek". Danish readers distrust enthusiasm; restraint reads as confidence.

  2. A number instead of an adjective

    Not "hurtigt" but "12.14". Not "omfattende" but a count. Every adjective that could be a figure should be one — and if there is no figure, question whether the adjective is true.

  3. Say the unglamorous thing

    "Det meste vil være, som det skal være. Det er også et svar." Admitting that most results are unremarkable is the most trust-building sentence on the site, and no competitor will write it.

  4. Never promise an outcome

    Pandonia measures and explains. It does not prevent, optimise, unlock or transform. Their existing disclaimer — "vi kan ikke love immunitet" — survives the rewrite intact.

Banned outright

Tag kontrol over dit helbred · unlock · optimér dit potentiale · den bedste version af dig selv · biohacking · game changer · revolutionerende · vidste du at… · "i en verden hvor" · any fear statistic about disease · any claim that a marker prevents anything · any before/after framing · "nemt og enkelt" (says nothing) · "vi tror på" (nobody asked) · exclamation marks anywhere outside an error.

Danish-specific trap: avoid the translated-English rhythm of "Med Pandonia får du…" and "Vi hjælper dig med at…". Danish marketing Danish is recognisable and it undercuts the premium position instantly. Write sentences that could appear in a well-edited Danish magazine, not in a newsletter.

Fixed in the prototype: --ink3 darkened from #8A8175 to #756C60. It carries every mono label, unit, axis value and caption on the site — at 3.4:1 all of that failed AA, and at 10–11px it is not large text. One token, every instance.

New system rule: on deep forest, 0.55 is the minimum opacity for any text. The 0.45 values used for secondary labels fail and must be raised. Phase 2 should adopt both changes.

The rest

Colour is never the only signal

The range band encodes state three ways: position of the tick, a text label ("Inden for området"), and colour last. A reader with any colour vision deficiency loses nothing — the position and the words carry it. This is the single most important accessibility decision in the system and it was made on design grounds first.

Native semantics

<details> for every marker and every FAQ item — keyboard, screen reader and browser find-in-page work with no code. Function's equivalent rows are plain buttons with no state. We get more by writing less.

Focus

A single global :focus-visible rule: 2px forest outline, 3px offset. Never removed, never replaced with a colour change alone.

Type sizes

Body never below 16px. Mono labels never below 10px and never carrying meaning that is not repeated elsewhere. Line length capped at 68 characters.

Reduced motion

Implemented first, not retrofitted. All transitions collapse to 0.001ms; the day timeline renders complete; the band tick is already in place.

Forms

The filter carries a real <label for>, visible rather than a placeholder. Errors state what to do. Open the booking form is on a third-party platform we do not control — an audit of EasyPractice is a separate task.

Touch targets

Marker summaries and FAQ rows exceed 48px through padding. To fix inline editorial links are ~24px tall; they need vertical padding to reach 44px without changing their appearance.

Structure

One <h1> per page, no skipped levels, landmarks for nav/main/footer, and a skip link to #main. To add the skip link is not in the prototype.

Language

lang="da" on the document, lang="en" on the English product names that stay untranslated — Health Test, Female hormones — so a screen reader pronounces them correctly.

10 / Perf

Performance constraints

The identity is type, ground and photography. Only one of those three is expensive.

Budget

Fonts< 110 KB4 files — see below

Page weight, homepage< 900 KBEleven beats, five image moments

Rules

Four font files, no more

Serif Regular, serif Italic, Degular Regular, Degular Medium, plus DM Mono Regular. Subset to Latin + Danish diacritics. font-display: swap with a metric-matched fallback so the swap does not shift layout. No variable axes we do not use.

AVIF, with a WebP fallback

Two formats, four widths (390 / 780 / 1200 / 1800), srcset + sizes on every image. Separate art-directed crops for mobile via <picture> — the recrops in the shot list are a performance feature as well as a design one.

Lazy load everything below the fold

loading="lazy" and decoding="async" on every image except the hero, which gets fetchpriority="high" and a preload.

No video

Nothing in the art direction needs it. If a lab clip is ever wanted: muted, 6 seconds, poster frame, preload="none", and never autoplaying on mobile.

Animation budget

Transform and opacity only — never width, height, top or left. One orchestrated sequence per page (the day timeline) and at most two slow image scales. Everything runs on the compositor.

The expensive effects, named

Three things would cost the most and none is essential: the drifting marker marquee (drop it on mobile entirely), the slow hero image scale (pure transform, cheap, keep), and the hero's tall 3:4 crop, which is the single largest asset on the site and needs the tightest encoding.

No framework needed

The prototype is static HTML with 40 lines of JavaScript. A Next.js build is reasonable for content management and routing, but nothing in this design requires client-side rendering — and the marker library is the ideal case for static generation.

The gradient placeholders are not the plan

The CSS-gradient photography in the prototype costs nothing and ships nothing. Real images replace them one for one; the layout dimensions do not change.

11 / Benchmark

Benchmark audit

Against Superpower, Function, Function's What We Test, and Celto. Not scored — located.

Where Pandonia shares category conventions

Warm off-white ground

Function, Celto and now Pandonia. Unavoidable and correct — the alternative is the clinical blue everyone spent a decade escaping.

A serif display face

Function and Pandonia. Shared, but the faces differ and Pandonia's is paired with a grotesk interface layer that Function does not have.

Included / add-on state per marker

Taken from Function, deliberately. It is the right pattern and there is no more distinctive way to say the same thing.

Price stated plainly, early

All four do it. Hiding price behind a form is the only real alternative and it is worse.

Where Pandonia deliberately differs

Forest green

The category converged on rust — Function #B05A36, Superpower #FC5F2B, Celto terracotta. Pandonia is the only one anchored in green, and it is the single most recognisable decision in the system.

The range band as an identity

Nobody else draws one object at three scales for everything numeric. Function shows no ranges and no units at all on What We Test.

Two taxonomy layers

Everyone else organises by organ. Pandonia navigates by health area and explains by biological system — and never lets the second one become a menu.

The day as a structure

Every competitor's story is annual. Pandonia's fits between breakfast and the drive home, and the timeline is the only section on the site that could not be lifted anywhere else.

A limits section

"Hvad vi ikke måler." No benchmark has one.

Refusing to congratulate

No green ticks for being in range, no score celebration, no streaks. Superpower has seventeen health scores and a glowing body diagram; Pandonia has a tick on a band.

Where anything still feels derivative

The index bar. A sticky category navigation above a scrolling marker list is Function's skeleton. Ours is 50px instead of a 300px rail, uses text rather than pills, and carries six items instead of twenty-four — but the idea is theirs.

Acknowledged

The marker list itself. Name, meaning, state, expand. There is no radically different way to list biomarkers, and inventing one would be novelty rather than design.

Accepted

The price rows. Two products, descriptions, figures. Thin differentiation — but a price table is not where a brand should fight for distinctiveness.

Accepted

Nothing else survives the test. Drop the day timeline, the model chapter, the limits section or the hero into Function or Superpower and they would be incoherent — wrong country, wrong timescale, wrong taxonomy, wrong claim.

Clear

The recognisable signature

If someone saw one screen with the logo removed, four things would identify it: forest green on cream; a serif headline against a mono fact line; a sage band with an amber tick; and a Danish sentence with a full stop in it. That is a signature — four elements, each doing a different job, none borrowed whole.

13 / Review

Creative director review

Only findings that matter. Three are fixed above; four are open and two of those need your decision.

Does it feel unmistakably Pandonia? Yes — see the four-element signature in §11. The green, the band and the Danish are not borrowable.

Yes

Is Dagslys × Nulpunkt still one coherent idea? Yes, and it survived contact with eight pages. Every Nulpunkt section carries a serif line or a photograph; every Dagslys section carries a mono fact. The clearest proof is the hero: 55/45, and it reads warm.

Yes

Is the homepage memorable? The hero and the day timeline are. Beats 07 and 08 are the weakest stretch — two deep-forest data sections in a row, and the second is mostly placeholders. Once the real scores exist it will hold; today it is the flattest part of the page.

Mostly

Is the science understandable? The structure is, and the vitamin-D opening teaches the reader how to read the page before asking them to. But almost none of the science is written yet, so this cannot honestly be answered until the clinical content arrives.

Untestable

Premium without being luxurious for its own sake? Yes. The premium signals are restraint, single weights and whitespace — not gold, not serif flourish, not a hero video.

Yes

Credible without being clinical? Yes, and the two-state band is why. Refusing to grade every value is what separates this from a lab portal.

Yes

Copenhagen without tourism? Yes — the city appears as postcodes, a street name and a kitchen. No harbour, no Nyhavn, no bicycle-in-golden-hour. Watch the shoot; this is the easiest thing to lose on the day.

Yes

Is the photography doing enough? Cannot be answered from placeholders. Placement is right — five image moments on the homepage, one per area chapter, three in the lab. But the site is designed around photography it does not have, and that is the largest single risk in the project.

Open risk

Too text-heavy? Om Pandonia is. Three consecutive text blocks with one image between them — the most conventional page in the set. It needs either a second photograph between "Hvorfor hjemme" and "Hvorfor vores eget laboratorium", or one of the three sections cut.

Needs work

Is any page unnecessary? No — but FAQ is the one at risk. If its answers grow into full explanations it will duplicate three other pages. The responsibility map holds it to short answers plus a link.

Watch

Does mobile feel designed? Yes. Four components are genuinely redesigned rather than reflowed, and two of those — the hero and the day timeline — are better on a phone than on a desktop.

Yes

Is the speed story repeated too often? It was. The process page retold the homepage timeline verbatim. Fixed. Site-wide the clock now appears once (homepage), the day as stages once (process page), and "samme dag" three times total across eight pages.

Fixed

Accidentally copying Function or Superpower? The index bar is Function's skeleton and I have said so rather than pretending otherwise. Everything else diverges. Nothing is a copy at component level.

One, named
