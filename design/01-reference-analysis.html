00 / Baseline

What Pandonia already is

From pandonia.com as it stands today, plus what your concepts confirm.

The proposition

34 biomarkers per draw. One composite Pandonia Score plus six category scores. A 20-minute online doctor consultation. 3,500 DKK for the test, 4,750 DKK with the consultation, or a membership.

The two facts nobody else has

A phlebotomist comes to your home or workplace — five minutes, central Copenhagen and Frederiksberg. The report lands in two to four hours. Superpower quotes ten days. Function tests twice a year. Celto sends you to one of 40+ hospitals.

The story already in place

"Healthier in body, happier in soul, longer in life." A Blue Zones framing; a model built on the four horsemen of ageing. A named Danish clinical team — Deborah Saren, bioanalytiker; Benedikte Halle, læge — who already appear by name in your concepts.

What the live site does with it

Lowercase pandonia in Degular, DM Sans for text, an electric blue pill on light grey, a strip of undifferentiated image tiles. Competent; interchangeable. Nothing in the layout says four hours, at your kitchen table, in Copenhagen. Your concepts already fix this.

01 / Benchmarks

The three benchmarks, read closely

Read live, with type and colour measured off the running page. For each: what works, what transfers, what would be theft, and the one principle to carry.

Superpower

superpower.com

A membership brand wearing luxury-consumer clothes. The health is almost never on screen — the life is.

What works

  • One typeface, one weight. NB International Pro at 400 everywhere — display, body, buttons. Authority from scale and tracking, never from bold.
  • H1 at 56px on a 56px line, tracking −0.02em. The headline reads as a block of material, not a sentence.
  • Full-bleed black opener, type low-left over photography. No hero card, no centred stack.
  • Numbers with receipts: 60% found something a doctor missed, survey n=143 printed underneath.
  • Testimonials written as headlines with a name and an age, not floating quote marks.

Transfers to Pandonia

  • Single-weight discipline — the cheapest premium signal there is, and it survives Danish.
  • Price framed against an alternative. 3,500 DKK needs a private-clinic comparison beside it.
  • Real members, named and aged, photographed as people rather than patients.

Do not copy

  • The dashboard-on-a-tablet shot — the most reproduced image in the category.
  • The glowing green organ on a grey body diagram. Sci-fi anatomy is the "futuristic biotech" you ruled out.
  • 9999px pill radii on everything; the vertical 01–04 index ticking beside a sticky section.
  • The influencer follower-count wall. Wrong register for Danish healthcare.

Principle to adopt

Set the site in one weight and let size, tracking and leading carry every level of hierarchy.

Function Health

functionhealth.com

The one reference that solves the problem you actually have: how to make a long list of lab tests feel like a library instead of a spreadsheet.

What works

  • Three typefaces, three jobs: Financier Display for voice, FT Base for interface, Fragment Mono for machine facts. The mono is the trick — [CALCULATION DERIVED FROM LAB TESTS] in mono reads as a note from the lab, not marketing.
  • H1 at 80px, weight 300, line-height 0.9 — display set lighter than body.
  • Italic as editorial gesture: "What we test", "Ignored by most. Tested by us."
  • Warm palette in a category that defaults to cold blue.
  • Hero is a macro crop of repeated hands sliced into vertical bands. Tactile, art-directed, no doctor in sight.

Transfers to Pandonia

  • Mono-as-evidence. Directly transferable, costs nothing.
  • Light display weights. Danish reads well at 300 — calm authority instead of shouting.
  • Three voices with three jobs, rather than one family doing everything.

Do not copy

  • Cream + serif + terracotta is Function's signature — and half the AI-generated web's. Take the structure, change the accent.
  • Financier Display itself. Using Function's exact face is the one unforced error available here.
  • The floating result cards drifting over the hero photo. Decorative; they fight the image.
  • The scale. 160 tests justifies an encyclopaedia. Borrowing that layout for 34 makes 34 look thin.

Principle to adopt

Give clinical fact its own typographic voice — a monospace register reading as instrumentation, sitting quietly beside an editorial one.

Function — Hvad vi måler

/what-we-test

Structurally the most useful page in the set. Strip the styling and the skeleton is worth taking outright.

The actual mechanics

  • Teaser before taxonomy. Four unexpected markers scroll past — Lead, ANA Screen, Lipase, Omega-3 — each with a plain-language gloss, under "Ignored by most. Tested by us." You're sold on depth before you're shown scale.
  • Two ways in. Search for people with a word in mind; a category rail for people who came to browse.
  • The count is in the heading. "Heart (15)", "Nutrients (19)". You always know how much sits behind a label.
  • Three states per marker. Included / tested 2× a year / add-on. One list encodes the commercial model without a pricing table.
  • Honest annotation. Derived values marked [CALCULATION DERIVED FROM LAB TESTS]. Admitting what's computed rather than measured buys more trust than any badge.
  • Sticky rail, scrolling detail. You never lose your place in 160 items.

Transfers to Pandonia

  • The full sequence: surprise → orient → category → marker → what it means for me.
  • Counts in headings; a state system per marker (included / add-on / membership).
  • Six areas is an advantage: six doors is navigable without a search field. Function needs search because it has twenty-four.

Do not copy

  • Twenty-four categories. Don't inflate six to look comprehensive.
  • The row-of-pills chip rail — functional, generic, on your avoid-list.
  • The endless single-column accordion. Six areas can each afford a real editorial page.

Principle to adopt

Open with the marker nobody expects, not the total. It's the only framing where 34 beats 160.

Celto

celto.dk

Worth flagging before anything else: not a mood reference. A Danish blood-testing company selling to the same person in the same city.

What works

  • The headline is the product: "Tjek dit helbred." Three words, a full stop, 72px. Danish directness as a design asset.
  • Three routes stated plainly — targeted panel, one annual check, build your own — each with a "from" price.
  • A quiz placed exactly where doubt appears.
  • Credibility as plain numbers in the hero: 86 tests, 40+ hospitals, doctor-interpreted.
  • A warm, close-cropped skin photograph — neck and jaw, golden light, no face.

Transfers to Pandonia

  • Danish copy that states the offer flatly beats translated American aspiration. Keep the Danish blunt — your concept's "Kend din krop. Før den siger til." already does this better than Celto.

  • The three-equal-cards choice section and the icon-led "why choose us" triptych.
  • Their framing — hospital laboratories, 40+ locations. Don't compete on their axis.

Principle to adopt

Take the Danish plain-speaking. Avoid the accent hue, not the warmth.

Your three references

Ref 01 — the hero concept

"Kend din krop. Før den siger til."

This is the strongest thing in the whole set, and it is already Pandonia. Note what it stops doing: no blue, no lowercase Degular wordmark, no grey.

What makes it work

  • The green is the whole identity. #415A3B is a true forest green — blue in it, not olive. It carries the headline, the wordmark and the CTA. Nobody in this category owns green.
  • Serif wordmark, capital P. A complete reversal of the current lowercase sans mark, and it buys decades of implied history in one gesture.
  • The headline is a promise, not a description. "Før den siger til" — before it tells you — sells prevention in four words without a single fear word.
  • The subhead does the entire proposition in one breath: home, Copenhagen, 5 minutes, 34 biomarkers, same day, a doctor if you want one. That's the whole business model in 38 words.
  • Split hero, type left, image right. Not full-bleed, not centred. Reads editorial rather than campaign.

Your note: "billedet gøres lidt mere lækkert og levende"

Agreed, and here's the diagnosis. The photograph is correct — right age, right room, right light, right mood — but it's shot like stock: eye-level, safe mid-shot, whole subject in frame, everything sharp, nothing cropped.

What Function and Superpower do instead: they crop past the subject. Function slices hands into vertical bands; Superpower shoots a member from behind, out of focus, mid-thought. The image becomes a texture, not a portrait.

The fix, concretely: crop much closer — the hand and the mug, the light on the forearm, the window edge. Shoot into the light rather than with it. Let the frame cut the body. Shallow depth of field so one thing is sharp. Keep the woman, keep the kitchen, keep the morning — lose the middle distance.

What I'd change

  • The image corner radius. Softens something that would read better as a hard editorial crop, bleeding to the right edge of the viewport.
  • The CTA pill. A green rectangle with a real corner would sit better against a serif of this weight.
  • Nav is set in the same family as the headline — it should drop to the interface voice so the serif stays special.

Ref 03 — Function, "1000s of diseases"

functionhealth.com

This one answers the question you asked in point 5: how to make a number feel meaningful rather than competitive. It doesn't argue about volume — it shows one thing moving.

The mechanism

  • Volume becomes ambient texture. Two rows of disease names counter-scroll behind the focal object. You perceive the scale without reading a single one. Nobody is asked to process a list.
  • Depth is one small object in the centre. A hairline card showing one marker's journey: below range (rust, May 25) → in range (green, Oct 25) → a pulsing ring on the projection (May 26). A complete story in about 330 pixels.
  • Italic plus colour marks the emotional half of the sentence. "Monitor early indicators of 1000s of diseases". Two typographic devices doing one job.
  • "PAUSE MOTION" is visible. Accessibility shown rather than hidden in a media query. It reads as craft and respect, and it's free.
  • Three small sub-claims sit under the headline as a single quiet line, not three icon cards.

The Pandonia translation

Pandonia's version of this is stronger, because Function's timeline spans a year and yours spans an afternoon.

The proof object is one marker — say ferritin, or HbA1c — shown against its reference band, with the draw at 09:00 and the result at 13:00 on the same axis. The 34 markers exist as ambient texture around it. You never write "34" next to "160+". You show one number that moved, and let the rest be atmosphere.

This also becomes the answer for "Hvad vi måler": lead with the marker nobody expects you to measure and one sentence on what it can tell them — depth first, taxonomy second.

Do not copy

  • The rust/green in-range colour pair. Rust is Function's brand colour doing double duty as a semantic — we need semantics that aren't the brand accent.
  • Disease names as the scrolling texture. Naming 40 diseases is a fear register, and it's wrong for a calm Danish brand. Use biomarker names, or what they can tell you.
  • The rounded card floating in white space. Our version should sit on a rule, in the grid.

Principle to adopt

Scale as atmosphere, depth as focus. One marker moving proves more than thirty-four listed.

03 / Patterns

What everything agrees on

Six moves shared across the benchmarks and your own concepts. These are the quality bar, not the design.

  1. Display type is set light or regular, never bold

    Superpower runs 400 across the entire site; Function sets its 80px H1 at weight 300; your hero concept sets a serif at a normal weight and lets the size do the work. Bold display type is the loudest tell of a cheap health brand.

  2. Line-height at or below 1.0 on headlines

    56/56 at Superpower, 80/72 at Function, 72/72 at Celto. Tight leading turns a headline into a shape you read as one object. All of them pull tracking negative, around −0.02em.

  3. Photography is macro, cropped and human — never clinical

    Function crops hands into vertical bands; Celto shoots a neck with no face; Superpower shoots a member mid-thought. Not one shows a laboratory, a white coat, a clipboard or a molecule. Your hero has the right subject and the wrong crop — that's the gap you spotted.

  4. Trust is built from specifics, not seals

    "60%, n=143." "160+ tests." "40+ hospitals." "Deborah Saren, bioanalytiker." Every claim carries a number or a name. Nobody uses a trust badge.

  5. Complexity is revealed in layers, never dumped

    Category → count → marker → plain-language meaning → deeper article. Each layer is a choice the reader makes. The full list exists; nobody is forced through it.

  6. Price is confronted early and framed against an alternative

    "What could cost $10,000 is now $349." "$15,000 is $365." "Fra 1000 kr." None hides the number behind a contact form; none states it without a comparison beside it.

04 / Colour

Reassessing warm vs cool

You asked me not to lock this until I'd seen your references. Having seen them: I was wrong, and here's the corrected argument.

What I got wrong

I recommended cool daylight tones on the reasoning that Celto occupies warm Scandinavian space. That reasoning treats temperature as the axis of difference. It isn't — hue is. Going cool to avoid Celto would have thrown away the warmth that makes both of your concepts feel human, and won a distinction nobody would notice.

What the references show

All three warm brands sit in the same narrow band: a warm off-white ground with an orange-red accent. Function #B05A36. Celto, terracotta. Superpower #FC5F2B. The entire category has independently converged on rust.

Where Pandonia already is

Your concepts are warm — and the primary is #415A3B, a deep forest green, with amber #E3A754 as the state colour. That is a genuinely different signature within the warm family. Not one competitor uses green as a primary.

The decision

Warm ground, green primary, amber state. Keep the warmth; move the accent out of the rust band entirely. Green also earns its keep semantically — it's the Blue Zones story, Danish nature, and the colour of "in range" — without ever becoming mint-wellness green, because #415A3B is dark, desaturated and blue-leaning.

The risk I'm watching

Deep green on cream with a serif is also the natural-wine, olive-oil, apothecary look. §05 lists the four countermeasures that keep it a health-science brand.

05 / Decision

Creative direction decision

The system I would build Phase 2 on, pending your choice of territory in §06.

Visual territory

Scandinavian editorial, warm ground, scientific spine. The page reads like a well-made Danish annual report that happens to be about a body: generous, typographic, photographic — then tightens abruptly wherever real data appears. That rhythm contrast, airy editorial against dense instrument, is the signature. Nobody in this category does both; Superpower is all air, Function is all catalogue.

Colour

Two grounds, one primary, one ink, one state colour, two supports. Cream #F6F1E8 and sand #F4EADB alternate to give sections their own temperature. Skovgrøn #415A3B is the primary — wordmark, headlines, CTAs. Warm ink #26201A for text, brown-biased so it never reads as neutral black. Amber #E3A754 is a state colour only: selection, a value in range, the active step. Sage #A0B89F and clay #AE9D84 support. The existing electric blue is retired. No gradients anywhere.

Typography

Three voices with three jobs, borrowed structurally from Function but with different faces. A high-contrast serif for the editorial voice — headlines, the wordmark, pull quotes only. A humanist grotesk for interface, navigation and body. A monospace strictly for measured fact: biomarker names, units, reference ranges, timestamps. Set display at one weight, tracking −0.02em, leading at or below 1.0.

On the serif: not Financier — that's Function's. GT Sectra Display is my first candidate; it's cut with a scalpel edge rather than a broad nib, which is an unusually apt gesture for this brand. Canela is the safer alternative. Degular survives, demoted from display to interface — which answers your keep-or-replace question as keep, but give it a superior. That makes this a redesign with continuity rather than a rebrand.

Photography & art direction

Copenhagen domestic realism, cropped hard. Real apartments, real morning light from a north window, real ages, real bodies. The subject matter of your hero is exactly right; the treatment needs to change.

Four rules: crop past the subject — a forearm, a hand on a mug, the light along a collarbone, the window edge, never a full mid-shot; shoot into the light, not with it, so there's bloom and falloff; let one plane be sharp and the rest go; let the frame cut the body. Include the draw itself, undramatised — five minutes at a kitchen table is the most ownable image this brand has. Never a laboratory, a white coat, a clipboard, a molecule, a glowing organ or a stock smile.

Graphic & data language

Hairline, ranged, and honest. One weight of rule. Reference bands drawn as a tinted horizontal band with the value marked on it — the same object at every scale, from a single marker to the Pandonia Score. Amber for the current value, sage for in range, warm ink for the axis. No fills, no gradients, no donut charts, no dashboard furniture.

The recurring device is the time axis: draw at 09:00, report at 13:00, on one line. It's the clock that makes the brand, and it scales from the process section to a single marker's history.

What makes it uniquely Pandonia

Six things no competitor can take: the green, in a category that has converged on rust. The four-hour clock as a graphic device rather than a claim. Named Danish clinicians appearing as people, not credentials. Your own kitchen as the location, in every photograph. A warm ink instead of a neutral black. And 34 as a curated number with an argument behind it — the markers a Danish doctor would actually act on — never a smaller number apologising to a bigger one.

Provenance — what comes from where

From Superpower

  • One weight across the whole site; hierarchy from size, tracking and leading.
  • Headline leading at or below 1.0, tracking negative.
  • Claims carrying their own receipts (n=, %, source line).
  • Testimonials written as headlines with a name and an age.
  • Price framed against what it replaces.

From Function Health

  • Three type voices with three distinct jobs.
  • Monospace as the register of measured fact.
  • Display set lighter than body.
  • Depth before volume; the unexpected marker before the total.
  • Counts in category headings; a state system per marker.
  • Honest annotation of what's derived rather than measured.
  • Sticky navigation against scrolling detail.

From Celto

  • Danish plain-speaking — state the offer flatly, in Danish, with a full stop.
  • Numbers in the hero as credibility.
  • A route for the undecided placed exactly where doubt appears.
  • And one negative: their accent hue is a no-go zone.

From your references

  • The entire palette — green, cream, sand, amber, warm ink.
  • Serif wordmark, capital P.
  • "Kend din krop. Før den siger til." and the 38-word subhead.
  • Split editorial hero rather than full-bleed.
  • Cards only when they contain real product.
  • Real staff names as trust.
  • Amber as state, never decoration.
  • Register shift between editorial and working sections.
  • One deliberate inversion per sequence.
  • Scale as atmosphere, depth as focus.

Five things we must avoid

  1. The rust band

    Function, Celto and Superpower all accent in orange-red. Amber must stay yellow-gold at #E3A754 and never drift toward #B05A36. This is the single easiest way to lose the distinction we're buying.

  2. Cards holding prose

    The rule from your own preferred concept: a card may contain a piece of real product — a picker, a person, a score, a chart. It may never contain a paragraph with an icon on top. Everything else gets whitespace, rules and type.

  3. Serif everywhere

    The serif is the emotional voice only. Never interface, never navigation, never data, never a button. The moment it sets a table, the brand becomes a restaurant.

  4. The apothecary drift

    Green + cream + serif is also natural wine, olive oil and skincare. Four countermeasures: keep the green blue-leaning and dark, never sage; keep amber strictly semantic; make the data passages genuinely dense against the airy editorial ones; and shoot contemporary Copenhagen interiors, never styled still-life on linen.

  5. Competing on count

    Never put 34 beside 160+. Volume is Function's game and they've won it. Depth, speed and curation are ours — 34 markers a Danish doctor will actually act on, delivered before dinner.

06 / Territories

Three territories to choose between

All three sit on the same palette and type system decided in §05. What changes is the organising idea — and each produces a visibly different homepage.

Recommended Territory 01

Dagslys

Danish for daylight. Something that was dark and guessed at, brought into ordinary morning light — in the ordinary room you were already standing in, before the afternoon is over.

Warm now, not cool: this is the light in your hero photograph, which is exactly why it survived. Organising idea: proximity and clarity. Homepage consequence: photography-led, light and room as the recurring motif, the whole page built around the five minutes and the four hours. Strength: it's the only territory that makes the logistics emotional, and it's directly supported by both your concepts. Risk: "daylight" drifts toward generic lifestyle unless the data language stays hard.

Territory 02

Nulpunkt

Your zero point. The first draw is the line every later one is measured against — and the brand is built from the measuring instrument rather than the room.

Organising idea: the reference range. Homepage consequence: type- and rule-led rather than photo-led; the reference band becomes the page's structural grid; photography drops to sparse macro punctuation; the mono voice gets much more space. Strength: the most scientifically credible of the three, and the strongest foundation for "Hvad vi måler". Risk: it can turn cold and instrumental — it would need the photography working hard to stay human, which is the opposite of where your references lean.

Territory 03

Før

Before. Taken straight from your own headline — kend din krop, før den siger til. Health as foresight: the whole site is about the gap between now and the moment a body finally tells you.

Organising idea: time and trajectory. Homepage consequence: the page is structured as a timeline rather than a stack of sections; the dominant graphic is a line moving across time; large editorial dates and ages; photography of people in ongoing, unremarkable good health. Strength: connects directly to the Blue Zones and four-horsemen story you already have, and it's the most emotionally distinctive. Risk: foresight sits one word away from fear — it needs disciplined copy to stay calm, and Danish healthcare buyers are unforgiving about alarmism.

My recommendation

Dagslys, with Nulpunkt's instrument language used as the data system inside it — and "Før den siger til." kept as the headline regardless of which you pick.

Dagslys and Nulpunkt aren't really rivals — one is the emotional register, the other is the data register, and the brand needs both. Før is the genuine alternative: choosing it would change the homepage structure from rooms-and-light to a single timeline. Tell me if that's the more interesting brand and I'll build Phase 2 on it instead.
