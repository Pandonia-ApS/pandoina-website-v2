How to read this

"Dagslys" and "Nulpunkt" are internal names. They never appear on the website, in copy, or in a deck shown to a customer. They exist so that a design decision can be argued about in one word.

Dagslys is the register that makes Pandonia desirable — ground, light, photography, the serif voice, air. Nulpunkt is the register that makes it credible — numbers, ranges, mono, density, precision. Neither wins. The governing ratio is in §11.

Specimens here are rendered in stand-ins. GT Sectra Display and Degular are licensed faces not available to this document, so the serif shows as Newsreader and the sans as DM Sans. Sizes, weights, leading and tracking are the real specification. DM Mono is the specified face.

Usage rules

The ratio

Roughly 55% cream, 20% sand, 12% deep forest, 8% paper, 4% green, under 1% amber. If amber is visible more than twice in a viewport, something has gone wrong.

Amber is a pointer, not a paint

It marks where you are: your value on a scale, your chosen time slot, the current step. It never means good, bad, new, or important. It is never a text colour on cream — the contrast fails. Where amber must read as text, use ink and let the tick carry the colour.

Two clinical states, not three

Within range (sage) or outside range (brick). No amber-as-borderline, no five-step severity ramp. Consumer health reports over-grade; two states plus the position on the band is more honest and far easier to read at a glance.

Brick is rationed

Brick sits in the rust band the whole category has converged on. It is permitted only because a health site needs an unambiguous out-of-range signal. Hard limit: never a fill larger than 24px, never a background, never a button, never more than two instances per viewport. At that scale it reads as a clinical mark, not a brand accent.

Retired

The existing electric blue is out of the system entirely — including links, focus rings and form states. Nothing in the palette is blue.

Contrast floor

Ink on cream is 12.4:1. Ink 2 on cream 6.1:1. Ink 3 on cream 3.4:1 — mono labels only, never body text. Forest on cream 6.8:1. Cream on deep forest 11.9:1. Every combination in the system passes AA for its size; the one that only passes for large text is flagged in the table.

02 / Type

Typography

Three voices with three jobs. The serif is the brand's mouth, Degular is its hands, the mono is its instruments.

The three faces

GT Sectra Display — the editorial voice

Regular 400 and Italic 400. Nothing else. Never bold. Chosen over Function's Financier because Sectra is cut with a scalpel edge rather than a broad nib — an unusually apt gesture for a company that takes blood, and a face nobody else in this category uses.

Fallback if the licence is blocked: Newsreader (Google Fonts, optical size 60+). Meaningful quality drop — it's softer and more bookish. Flag before Phase 5, not after.

Degular — the interface voice

Regular 400 and Medium 500. Two weights, no more. Kept from the existing brand, demoted from display to interface. Navigation, body copy, buttons, labels, working-section headings, everything with a job to do.

Fallback: DM Sans, then system sans. Degular Text for anything under 20px, Degular Display above.

DM Mono — the instrument voice

Light 300 and Regular 400. Reserved absolutely for measured fact: biomarker names in a list, values, units, reference ranges, timestamps, section labels, honesty annotations. The moment mono is used for atmosphere it stops meaning "this was measured."

Free and open-source. It shares DM Sans's geometry, so it sits naturally against Degular.

The division rule

The serif owns everything above 44px. Degular owns everything below 24px. Between 24 and 44px the choice is by register: editorial and emotional → serif; working and functional → Degular.

Two consequences: the serif never appears at body size, where it would lose its character and start competing with Degular; and Degular never carries a hero, where it isn't distinctive enough to. A page that breaks this rule stops reading as Pandonia within one scroll.

Setting rules

Measure

Body 62–68ch, never past 72. Serif display 22–34ch depending on level. A serif headline that runs past three lines is the wrong size, not the wrong copy.

Case

Sentence case everywhere except mono labels, which are uppercase with +0.1em. No all-caps headlines. No all-caps buttons.

Numerals

Tabular in every table, band, axis and value. Proportional in running prose. Units always set 0.4× the value size in ink-3, never the same size as the number.

Balance

text-wrap: balance on every display level, text-wrap: pretty on body. Danish compounds are long — check sundhedsrapport and referenceområde at every breakpoint before signing a layout off.

Italic

The serif italic marks the emotional half of a sentence, once per section at most. Never for emphasis inside body copy — use ink weight, not slant.

Orphans

No single-word last lines on any display level. Hard-break the headline in the markup rather than hoping.

Three section densities — the pacing mechanism

Air

180px top and bottom desktop / 88px mobile. Editorial and photographic sections. One idea, one column, enormous surrounding space. This is where the brand feels expensive.

Standard

128px / 64px. Process, pricing, anything explanatory. Multi-element but not tight.

Dense

72px / 48px, with 16px internal rhythm. Nulpunkt sections: marker lists, bands, scores, tables. Deliberately tighter than everything around it — the compression is the signal that you've entered the instrument.

The pacing rule: no section may repeat the previous section's ground and density. Either the colour changes or the breathing changes — and ideally both, alternating. This single rule is what stops the page becoming a stack of identical bands, which is the most common failure mode in this category.

Asymmetry

Editorial blocks are offset

A serif headline with body sits on columns 1–6 or 3–8 or 6–12. Never 1–12 centred. Centred type is reserved for exactly one moment on the homepage — the closing statement — where it lands because nothing else was.

Text and image never split 50/50

Use 55/45 or 42/58. An even split reads as a template; an uneven one reads as a decision.

Vertical offset

In any two-part composition, the two parts never share a top edge. Offset by at least 64px, usually more.

The baseline

Type blocks sit on an 8px vertical grid. Images and full-bleed elements are exempt — they answer to the composition, not the grid.

04 / Photo

Photography art direction

This is the single largest gap between the current concept and the benchmark quality. The subject matter was already right; the treatment is what changes.

Light

One source, from a window

North-facing daylight, morning to early afternoon. No fill, no reflector, no flash, no continuous LED. If the room is dark, shoot it dark.

Shoot into it, not with it

Backlight and hard sidelight, with visible falloff across the frame and bloom where the window edge enters. The current hero is lit flatly from the front, which is exactly what makes it read as stock.

Let shadow exist

Deep shadow is permitted and encouraged. Lifted blacks in the grade, never crushed, never a flat "clean" exposure across the whole frame.

The five crops

Every shoot delivers against these five named setups. A shot that fits none of them does not go on the site.

HÅND

A hand and one object: a mug, a phone, a glass, the edge of a table. The most-used crop on the site. No face.

ARM

Forearm and inner elbow — the draw site — in raking light. Skin texture, hair, a vein, a small plaster afterwards. The most Pandonia image possible and nobody else can use it.

HALS

Neck, jaw, collarbone, shoulder. Human and identifiable without a portrait's self-consciousness.

RUM

The whole room with the person small and off-centre. Used at full bleed, once or twice per page, as the breath.

LYS

No person at all. Light across a wooden table, a curtain, steam off a cup, a radiator. Used as connective tissue between heavy sections.

Subjects, styling, location

Real Danish adults, 30–65

A genuine spread of age, body and gender. No model casting, no styling of hair or makeup beyond ordinary. Eyes to camera at most once on the entire site — and that one is the doctor.

Wardrobe inside the palette

Cream, sand, oatmeal, olive, warm grey, undyed linen and wool. No white shirts, no black, no logos, no pattern, no blue.

Real Copenhagen apartments

Worn parquet, a radiator under a window, a tiled kitchen, one plant, ceramic rather than glass. Never a set, never a rental studio dressed as a home, never a hotel.

The procedure, plainly

The draw shown as it is — a hand, a tourniquet, a kitchen table, five minutes. Undramatised, and never a needle in close-up. This is the sequence that proves the whole proposition, so it must be photographed properly rather than implied.

Banned

Laboratories. White coats. Scrubs. Stethoscopes. Clipboards. Microscopes. Petri dishes. Pipettes. DNA helices. Molecules. Glowing organs. Body diagrams. Pill bottles. Supplement flat-lays. Gym and fitness tropes. Running shoes. Smoothies. Someone smiling at the camera. Group shots of a "team" in a bright room. Before-and-after bodies. Anything shot on a white cyclorama. Any image where the light comes from more than one direction.

05 / Crop

Cropping and image treatment

Rules about the frame itself, which is where the concept's images lost most of their energy.

Square corners, always

Border radius on photography is 0. This is a hard change from the concept and the single cheapest upgrade available: a rounded photograph reads as a component, a square one reads as a plate in a magazine. Radius belongs to product cards only.

The frame cuts the body

Crop past the subject on at least two edges. If the whole person fits in the frame, it is the wrong crop. This is the specific fix for "lidt mere lækkert og levende".

At least one bleed per page

One image per page runs off a viewport edge — usually the right, occasionally both. Images that stop politely inside the grid on every section are what make a page feel like a template.

Overlap the ground change

An image may straddle the boundary between a cream and a sand section, half on each. This creates depth without a single shadow, and it makes the section transition an event rather than a seam.

No shadows. Ever.

Not on images, not on cards, not on buttons, not on the navigation. Depth comes from ground changes, bleed and overlap.

One permitted scrim

Where type must sit on a full-bleed image: a cream gradient, 0 → 60%, from the type's edge. Never a black scrim — it kills the warmth and instantly reads as a stock hero.

No filters, no duotone

Photographs are photographs. The grade is warm, lifted, slightly desaturated in the reds so skin stays natural. No teal-and-orange, no brand-colour overlay, no grain except an optional 2–3% on full-bleed heroes to kill the digital flatness.

Aspect ratios

4:5 in an editorial column. 3:2 at full bleed. 1:1 rarely, for a single portrait. 16:9 never — it is the aspect ratio of a template.

Mobile recrops, never rescales

Every image ships with a declared mobile crop, almost always tighter and more vertical. A desktop 3:2 squeezed into a phone loses the subject and the point.

06 / Graphic

Graphic language

Almost nothing. The identity comes from type, ground and photography — the graphic system exists only to keep those three honest.

One rule weight

1px, ink at 14%, at every scale. Section dividers are the same 1px, full-bleed. No 2px, no dashed, no double. On sand, rules take clay #AE9D84 instead so they don't go muddy.

No icon set

No Feather, no Lucide, no Material, nothing. The permitted marks are a 1px arrow →, a plus, and a chevron, drawn to match Degular's stroke. Never an icon inside a circle. Never an icon above a heading above a paragraph.

No anatomy illustration

If the body needs to be shown, it is photographed and cropped. No outline figures, no highlighted organs, no schematic torso.

The one proprietary mark

The reference band in §07. It is the only drawn object the brand owns, and it is used at three scales for everything numeric — which is what makes it an identity rather than a chart style.

Numbers carry units

Every number on the site carries its unit, and every clinical number carries its reference range or its source. A bare figure is not permitted, including in marketing sections.

The wordmark

Serif, capital P, set in skovgrøn on light grounds and cream on deep forest. One size relationship to the navigation, never scaled independently. No symbol, no lockup, no icon version beyond a favicon.

Anatomy

A 26px track with a 1px rule top and bottom. The reference range is a sage fill at 34% spanning the interval. Your value is a 2px amber tick running 5px past the track on both sides. The value sits above in mono, the range endpoints below in mono-S.

Outside the range

The tick turns brick and a 1px brick rule runs from the nearest band edge to the tick — so the eye measures how far, not just that. No red fill, no warning icon, no exclamation mark. The distance is the information.

Why not a gauge

Rings, donuts, dials and speedometers are dashboard furniture. They compress a continuous value into a shape you can't read precisely, and every health app has one. The band is precise, it scales from 64px to full width, and it's ours.

Chart rules

Trend over time

1px line in forest, amber dot on the latest point only, mono date labels beneath. No area fill, no gradient, no smoothing, no second axis. The reference band runs behind it as a horizontal sage strip.

Two colours maximum

Any chart uses ink plus at most two of amber, sage, brick. A chart needing more colours is two charts.

Banned chart types

Donut. Ring. Gauge. Speedometer. Pie. Radar. Stacked area. Bar chart with rounded ends. Anything 3D. Anything with a drop shadow.

Honesty annotations

Every chart is labelled

Unit, reference interval, and where the interval comes from. If a range differs by sex or age, that is stated on the chart, not in a footnote.

Tabular numerals

Everywhere a number sits above or beside another number. Non-negotiable — proportional figures in a column is the clearest sign nobody checked.

08 / UI

Buttons, navigation, core UI

Three button levels, a navigation that doesn't float, and one rule about cards that settles most arguments.

Buttons

Three levels · 2px radius · no shadow

The radius decision

2px, not a pill. This is a deliberate change from the concept. The full-radius pill is the single most category-generic shape on the web — Superpower, Celto and the current Pandonia site all use it. A near-square button under a serif wordmark reads editorial rather than app. Flag this one if you disagree; it's visible and it's reversible.

Metrics

Padding 15px / 28px. Degular Medium 16px. Primary: forest fill, cream text. Secondary: 1px forest border, no fill, inverts on hover. Tertiary: forest text with a 1px underline at 45% that reaches full opacity on hover.

States

Hover 140ms to #2C3F28. Focus: 2px forest outline, 3px offset — visible, never removed. Disabled: ink-3 text on a 6% ink fill, no border. No loading spinners inside buttons; the label changes to Sender… in mono.

Never

All-caps labels. Icons inside buttons except a trailing → on tertiary. Gradients. Shadows. More than one primary button in a viewport. A button wider than 320px.

Navigation

Desktop

72px tall. Serif wordmark left, Degular Regular 15px links, actions right. Transparent over the hero; after 80px of scroll it takes the cream ground and a 1px bottom rule. No blur, no glass, no shadow, no shrinking. Active item carries a 1px forest underline.

Mobile

60px tall. Wordmark and a two-line toggle. Open state is a full-screen deep-forest overlay with items set in the serif at D3 (27px), stacked with generous leading, and the CTA at the bottom. Not a white drawer with a list of 15px links — that is the default and it throws away the one moment mobile gives you to feel like the brand.

No sticky CTA bar

The persistent bottom bar is a conversion pattern, and it's generic. Instead the primary CTA reappears at the end of each of the three acts (§12), which is where someone is actually ready.

Fields and forms

Underline only · no box

Label above in mono-S. No border box, no fill, no radius — a 1px bottom rule that becomes a 2px forest rule on focus. Errors appear beneath in brick at 14px and say what to do, never just what is wrong. Booking is the one place a boxed field is permitted, because a date and time picker needs its own enclosure.

Cards — the single rule

A card may exist only to hold a piece of real product. A date picker. A named person with their role. A score. A marker with its band. An actual report extract.

A card may never hold prose. No icon-above-heading-above-paragraph. No three equal cards describing benefits. No "why choose us" triptych. Everything explanatory is carried by type, rules, ground and whitespace — which is most of the page.

Card spec when permitted: paper ground #FCFAF6, 8px radius, 1px rule at 8% ink, no shadow, 24px internal padding. Cards never sit in a row of four equal columns — see §12, beat 06, where the process becomes a timeline instead.

09 / Motion

Motion and interaction

One easing curve, one orchestrated moment per page, and everything visible at rest.

One curve, three durations

cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.6, 0.2, 1) for everything. Hover 140ms. Reveal 320ms. Orchestrated sequences 500–900ms. No bounce, no spring, no overshoot — the brand is calm, and easing is where calm is actually specified.

Everything is visible at rest

Reveals run from a visible state: 16px translate and opacity 0.6 → 1, staggered 60ms. Nothing is parked at opacity 0 waiting for an observer, and the first viewport never animates in. A shared link, a screenshot and a slow connection all have to show a finished page.

Image motion

A slow 1.06 → 1.00 scale across the scroll of its own section, or a mask that widens from the centre. Maximum 6% movement. Never parallax on text, never on more than two images per page.

The one orchestrated moment

The clock. As the process section enters, the line draws left to right over 900ms, the pips land, the amber dot arrives on the report time, and the elapsed figure counts up once. One moment, done properly, beats six scattered effects — and it's the moment that carries the proposition.

Numbers count once

Only where the number is the point: the Score, the elapsed time. Maximum two per page, 900ms, never on scroll-back.

The band animates in

The amber tick slides from the band's left edge to its position over 500ms, once, when the band first enters. It reads as a measurement being taken. Never loops.

The permitted marquee

One: a slow horizontal drift of biomarker names behind the featured marker, ~24s per loop, with a visible PAUSE control in mono — taken straight from Function, and the visibility of that control is half the point. Pauses on hover and on focus.

Reduced motion

All transforms off, opacity only. Numbers render final. The clock draws instantly. The marquee stops. This is implemented first, not retrofitted.

Never

Scroll-jacking. Page-transition overlays that delay content. Loaders on a static page. Autoplay video with sound. Cursor followers. Text that types itself. Anything that moves while being read.

10 / Mobile

Mobile design principles

Most of this traffic will be a phone in a kitchen. Four sections are redesigned rather than reflowed.

Redesigned, not reflowed

Hero inverts

Desktop is type-left, image-right. Mobile is type first, image second, full-bleed edge to edge at 4:5, with the mono fact strip between them. The serif drops to 44px and hard-breaks over three lines maximum.

The process becomes a timeline

The four-step row of cards becomes a vertical timeline with the clock running down the left edge — times on the rail, content to the right. This is genuinely better than the desktop layout, and it's a candidate for replacing the desktop version too.

Bands go full width

Marker name above, band full width, value and state below. Never side-by-side under 400px. Micro-bands in lists stay, at 48px instead of 64px.

Marker lists expand in place

One marker per row, tap to open the standard band beneath it, in the flow. No modal, no new page, no bottom sheet.

Rules

Ground alternates more often

In a narrow column, sections read as one continuous scroll unless the ground changes. Roughly every second beat on mobile against every third on desktop.

Density compresses, contrast holds

180 / 128 / 72 becomes 88 / 64 / 48. The ratio between air and dense is what carries the pacing, and it must survive the compression.

Images recrop

Every image has a declared mobile crop, tighter and more vertical. HÅND and ARM crops work at any width; RUM usually needs a different frame entirely.

Touch targets

44px minimum, achieved with padding rather than fill, so the visual weight stays light. Tap states are instant colour, never a ripple.

Tables scroll, pages don't

The body never scrolls horizontally. Wide marker tables get their own overflow-x container with a visible edge fade.

Performance budget

LCP under 2.0s on 4G. Hero image under 180KB in AVIF. Fonts: two weights of Degular, one of the serif, one mono — subset to Latin Extended for Danish. Total under 120KB. No font swaps visible above the fold.

11 / Both

How Dagslys and Nulpunkt coexist

The governing ratio, then three worked examples on the same page.

Dagslys sets the ground and the voice. Nulpunkt owns everything with a number in it.

And the rule that stops either from taking over: every Nulpunkt section carries at least one serif line or one photograph; every Dagslys section carries at least one mono fact. 13 / Rules

Ten hard rules

Each one exists because breaking it is how this site would become generic. If a design decision conflicts with one of these, the rule wins or the rule gets formally changed.

  1. No section repeats the previous section's ground and density

    Either the colour changes or the breathing changes, ideally both. This one rule does more against the stacked-identical-bands failure than everything else combined.

  2. The serif above 44px, Degular below 24px

    Between them, register decides: editorial → serif, working → Degular. A page that breaks this stops reading as Pandonia within one scroll.

  3. Photography has square corners, always

    Radius belongs to product cards and nothing else. A rounded photograph reads as a component; a square one reads as a plate in a magazine.

  4. A card holds product, never prose

    A picker, a person, a score, a band, a report extract. Never icon-above-heading-above-paragraph, never a row of equal cards describing benefits.

  5. Never three equal columns

    Any three-up grid of matched blocks — features, benefits, values, reasons — is banned outright. Use an offset editorial block, a list on rules, or a timeline.

  6. Amber is a pointer, never a paint

    It marks where you are. It is never decoration, never a judgement, never a text colour on cream, and never visible more than twice in a viewport.

  7. No shadows, no gradients, no glass, no glow

    Depth comes from ground changes, bleed and overlap. Maximum radius anywhere on the site is 8px, on product cards only.

  8. No icon sets, no anatomy illustration, no molecules

    Permitted marks: arrow, plus, chevron, drawn to Degular's stroke. If the body must be shown, it is photographed and cropped.

  9. Every number carries its unit and its reference

    Clinical figures carry range and source; marketing figures carry their basis. A bare number is not permitted anywhere, including a hero.

  10. One orchestrated motion moment per page

    Everything else is a 320ms reveal from a visible resting state. Nothing loops, nothing moves while being read, and the first viewport never animates.

On speed: proximity and same-day results stay in the system as evidence, never as the pitch. They appear as the clock, as real timestamps, as the kitchen-table photography, and as one mono line in the hero. Pandonia is not a fast-testing brand — it is a premium health brand whose experience happens to be effortless, and the clock is how that gets proven rather than claimed. If a layout starts shouting about speed, cut it back to the timestamps.
